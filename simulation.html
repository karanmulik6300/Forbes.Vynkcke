Calculation

GCV(AR) = 1/100 × [8080C + 34500(H − O/8) + 2240S]
NCV(AR) = GCV(AR) − 587 × [9×(H/100) + M/100]
Steam/Fuel = NCV × 4.1868 × Efficiency ÷ (Steam Enthalpy − Feedwater Enthalpy)
Fuel Required = Target Steam Capacity ÷ Steam/Fuel Ratio

GCV uses Dulong's formula on the as-received ultimate analysis. The 587 kcal/kg NCV deduction is the latent heat carried away by moisture and by the water formed when hydrogen burns. 4.1868 converts kcal to kJ.

New to this? Start here

GCV
Total heat released if all moisture ends up as liquid water.
NCV
Usable heat — moisture and combustion water leave as vapour instead, so this is always lower than GCV.
Steam/Fuel
How many tonnes of steam you get per tonne of fuel burned.
As received
The fuel exactly as it's weighed at the plant, moisture included.
Excess air
Air supplied above the bare minimum needed to burn the fuel completely — some extra is always needed so nothing goes unburnt.
Theoretical air
The bare minimum air the fuel's chemistry needs to burn completely, with zero extra.
Dry flue gas
The hot gas leaving the chimney, not counting the water vapour in it — this is what carries away the biggest single heat loss.
L1–L5 losses
The five ways a boiler loses heat: hot flue gas, water vapour from hydrogen, moisture in the fuel, moisture in the air, and radiation/unburnt fuel. Efficiency is 100% minus all five added up.
Indirect efficiency
Efficiency worked out from measured losses, as done in a real field trial — more trustworthy than a number typed in from memory.
CO₂ per tonne fuel
How much carbon dioxide burning one tonne of this fuel releases, based on its carbon content alone.
